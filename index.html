Our Story

Recipes Guarded
for Generations

Auntie Gaik Lean first learned Nyonya cooking at her mother's side at the age of twelve. Every recipe she carries — every blend of spice paste, every measure of tamarind — was passed down not from a cookbook, but from memory, from touch, from love.

Today, from a beautifully restored shophouse on Bishop Street in the heart of George Town's UNESCO World Heritage zone, she still oversees every single dish that leaves her kitchen. Nothing is compromised. Nothing is shortcut.

"The Michelin star didn't change how we cook. It simply told the world what we already knew."

Our Heritage

The Soul of
Peranakan Cooking

Peranakan cuisine is the living legacy of Chinese settlers who wove their traditions into the fabric of Malay culture along the Straits. The result is one of the world's most complex, layered, and labour-intensive culinary traditions.

At Auntie Gaik Lean's, nothing comes from a packet. The gulai tumis paste is blended from eight fresh ingredients — including fresh saffron. The nasi ulam is hand-tossed with over ten herbs. Every dish is an act of preservation.
