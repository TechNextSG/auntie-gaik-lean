The Beginning

Beh Gaik Lean —
Started Cooking at Twelve

Beh Gaik Lean (known as Auntie Gaik Lean) grew up in a Peranakan household in George Town, Penang, where the kitchen was the beating heart of the home. At twelve, she stood beside her mother at the heavy stone pestle, learning to grind rempah — the fragrant spice paste that forms the soul of every Nyonya dish. There were no written recipes. Everything was taste, touch, and memory.

Over more than fifty years, she honed her craft — including years in industrial kitchens at Motorola, Intel, Seagate and Dell, where she developed an uncompromising precision and consistency. She was later mentored by the late Datuk Lim Bian Yam, a celebrated Nyonya chef and Penang's Living Heritage Treasure awardee, before serving as head chef at a Nyonya restaurant in KL.

In 2013, her son Adrian Tan established the restaurant at 1 Bishop Street, George Town, giving Auntie Gaik Lean's cooking a permanent home. Daughter-in-law Jayna Tang also works alongside her in the kitchen. In December 2022, the inaugural Michelin Guide Malaysia awarded the restaurant one Michelin Star — making Auntie Gaik Lean the first Malaysian woman to receive the honour, at the age of 69. The star has been retained every year since.

Heritage

The Soul of
Peranakan Cooking

Peranakan — or Nyonya — cuisine is one of the world's great culinary traditions, born from the intermarriage of Chinese settlers with local Malay communities along the Straits of Malacca. What emerged was a cuisine of extraordinary complexity: Chinese techniques married with Malay spices, aromatics and ingredients, producing dishes that exist nowhere else on earth.

Penang Nyonya cooking is its own sub-tradition — sharper, sourer and spicier than its Singaporean and Malaccan cousins. The rempah paste changes with every cook. The balance of tamarind, belachan, candlenut and chilli is a deeply personal calculation, passed not in measurements but in the feel of the stone pestle and the colour of the wok.

At Auntie Gaik Lean's, the cooking is unreformed and uncompromised. The rempah is ground each morning. The stocks are made from bones, not powder. The kuih are steamed fresh. This is Nyonya cooking as it has always been — and as it must always remain.

Where to Find Us

1 Bishop Street,
George Town

Auntie Gaik Lean's occupies a lovingly restored pre-war shophouse on Bishop Street — one of Penang's most historic thoroughfares, in the heart of the UNESCO World Heritage Zone. The shophouse has barely changed since Auntie first opened its doors: the tiled floors, the high ceilings, the long wooden tables. It is a setting that feels like a memory. Everything here is intentional, and everything is old. That is exactly the point.

The Family

A Legacy
Shared Together

The restaurant is a true family endeavour. Son Adrian Tan established the eatery in 2013, providing Auntie Gaik Lean's cooking with a home worthy of its heritage. Daughter-in-law Jayna Tang works alongside in the kitchen, ensuring that every dish carries the same care and precision that has defined the restaurant from the beginning.

Auntie Gaik Lean's is one of only two Michelin-starred Peranakan establishments in the world — a distinction the family shares with pride and humility.

"I share this star with the vibrant Chinese Peranakan community and with all my fellow Penangites — it is not mine alone."

— Auntie Gaik Lean

Our Values

Kitchen Philosophy

Nothing from a Packet

Every paste, every stock, every sauce is made in-house. No pre-mixed powders, no commercial spice blends. Ever.

Made Fresh Every Morning

Preparation begins before dawn. By opening time, every dish has been freshly cooked and nothing has been sitting overnight.

Family Recipes Only

Every dish on the menu has been cooked in this family for generations. Nothing is invented for trend, and nothing is borrowed from elsewhere.

50+ Years of Mastery

Auntie Gaik Lean has been perfecting these dishes for over five decades. The Michelin star is a reflection of that — not its cause.
